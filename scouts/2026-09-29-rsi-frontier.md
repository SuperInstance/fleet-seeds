## §1 Weco deep-dive — AIDE²: the first claimed evidence of RSI

**Who weco is.** A research-and-product lab ("We build recursively self-improving AI", weco.ai). Platform: "production-grade autoresearch to your codebase, optimizing your code against any metric you define." Open-source lineage: WecoAI/aideml (AIDE, the ML-engineering agent that took first place in OpenAI MLE-Bench, arXiv:2502.13138). Stated doctrine — notable kinship with the embassy: "The world is an infinite-horizon game, so we contribute to the community and trade lessons in the open. We think that's the only way to build RSI that stays aligned."

**AIDE² (blog 2026-07-14, tech report arXiv:2609.26457).** Bi-level optimization: an OUTER autoresearch loop (their hand-tuned agent, claude-opus-4.7) rewrites the harness code of the INNER autoresearch loop (AIDE₀ on gemini-3-flash). 100 outer steps, 8 unattended days, AIDE₀→AIDE₉₉; seven successive versions improved under a matched cost budget; AIDE₄₇/AIDE₈₅ beat the 2-year hand-tuned AIDE_human on held-out and out-of-distribution tasks. Their honest claim: Level 1 of 0–3 on their RSI ladder, "we are not near an intelligence explosion with the current system," and the efficiency claim is "not statistically significant."

**The evaluation design is the real contribution** (most of their design effort, by their own account):
1. **Public/private score split** — the inner agent optimizes a public score; survival of any candidate is decided by a private score it cannot see. First-order generalization = leading on data you could not see. Reward hacking, "in this vocabulary," is a first-order generalization failure.
2. **Fixed cost budget per evaluation, metered in dollars** — "The fixed budget doubles as selection pressure, forcing algorithmic invention over brute force"; best-of-N↑ and parallel-wall-clock tricks cannot pass. A gain must be an efficiency improvement.
3. **Task heterogeneity** (ML engineering / heuristic algorithm engineering / harness engineering) — pressure toward generalizable algorithms, away from task-specific prompts.

**What the loop discovered (AIDE₈₅):**
- **Bandit-lineage search**: not MCTS (tried, rejected); each draft subtree is a multi-armed-bandit arm, greedy parent selection within a lineage, and on stall it FORKS the global best under a fresh strategy funded as a new arm.
- **16× context compression**: aggressive per-operator minimal context ("very different from the main trend of putting as much context as possible"); saved tokens reinvested as extra search steps.
- **A 3-layer anti-reward-hacking stack** it was never instructed to build: prompt instruction + hard-coded regeneration guard + a statistical outlier-removal layer. Emergent outcome: cheating rate 63% (AIDE₀) → 34% (AIDE₈₅), better than the hand-tuned 42% — selected purely because public-gamers don't survive the private score.
- **Eval repair**: the loop monkey-patched a broken private eval — fixing it rather than exploiting it.
- **Mutation fragility**: the statistical anti-hacking layer was implemented correctly earlier in the lineage and BROKEN by a later mutation (so its effect is zero in AIDE₈₅). Self-improvement without predecessor regression silently loses mechanisms.

**§1.1 What did NOT work (their §2.5 — the negative-results table).** 95 rejected proposals hand-read in one seed; they cover "a surprising share of the literature." Under the fixed budget with run-to-run noise ≈0.02–0.045: island populations with migration/crossover (−0.021, worse than incumbent), pairwise LLM-judge tournament selection (−0.090), stagnation-triggered exploration escalation (−0.040), restart policies (−0.078), majority-vote ensembles (−0.031, 3 variants), epsilon-annealing (−0.006, within noise), UCB-V variance-adaptive boosts (−0.080), MCTS value backup / ancestor trend propagation (−0.004, within noise), optimizer's-curse near-tie overrides (−0.007, within noise). The one genuine novelty: treating heavy revisits of one node as overfitting suspicion (−0.004, within noise). Verdict in their words: "the algorithm the loop eventually discovered is a composition of rather simple mechanisms."

**§1.2 Ignition test (Level 2).** Installing the discovered improver AIDE₄₇ into the outer-loop seat: it reaches the training ceiling in ~20 outer steps vs ~40 for AIDE_human — more sample-efficient but not asymptotically better; "we do not think this is strong enough evidence of ignition." Third-order generalization (better improver of improvers) NOT achieved.

**§1.3 Living with code you didn't write (§3.2).** The evolved agent is hard to understand, has dead code, and resists production deployment (visualization/steerability compatibility). Their open question: treat modular code as a black box and invest in interface design to handle autonomous knowledge velocity.

## §2 The wider frontier (papers + trending repos)

- **AIDE (original, arXiv:2502.13138)** — tree search over solution space; drafting/debugging/improving operators; experience-based backtracking; first place MLE-Bench.
- **GEPA (arXiv:2507.19457)** — reflective prompt evolution: natural language is a richer learning medium than scalar policy gradients; samples trajectories, reflects in text, proposes updates, and **combines complementary lessons from the Pareto frontier** of its own attempts; beats GRPO-class RL with ~35× fewer rollouts.
- **ADAS / Meta Agent Search (arXiv:2408.08435)** — agents defined in code; a meta agent programs ever better agents; programming languages are Turing-complete, so the search space is open-ended.
- **Absolute Zero (arXiv:2505.03335)** — ONE model proposes tasks AND solves them under verifiable rewards; zero human data; the proposer is rewarded for tasks at the learnability frontier.
- **"The Last AI Built by Humans" / Metan (arXiv:2609.11873)** — RSI roadmap of autonomy rungs: improvement-execution → improvement-strategy → experience-acquisition → environment-adaptation → recursive meta-improvement; Headroom-Closed Index to measure headroom; scenario-specific speeds (science / embodied / software).
- **"Simple Baselines are Competitive with Code Evolution" (OpenReview, Robert Lange co-authored circle)** — two simple baselines match or EXCEED sophisticated code-evolution pipelines in three domains (math bounds, agentic scaffolds, ML competitions); documents shortcomings in how code evolution is developed and evaluated. The field's own counter-signal, and the reason our dose-response/null-control discipline is an asset, not a superstition.
- **prime-agent (PrimeIntellect-ai, ★21.4k)** — self-improving RLM harness. Two abstractions: **RLM** ("prompt-as-a-variable": context treated as variables, recursive subagents as function calls, inside a persistent REPL) and **Continual Harness** (arXiv:2605.09998): prompts/memories/skills/subagent-specs as DURABLE STATE refined through small, evidence-backed updates, local to session by default.
- **reef (Human-Agent-Society, ★6.9k)** — "first open-source infrastructure for continual self-improving agents": connects inference → feedback → learning → versioned delivery; improves weights (Slime/SGLang) or harness (prompts/rules/skills).
- **Raven (EverMind-AI, ★4.3k)** — "Harness of Harnesses… built for RSI": runtime self-evolution where its own prompts, skills, agent loops and per-point judgement are replaceable, gated by verification ("a failed check sends it back").
- **HyperAgents (facebookresearch, ★2.8k)** — self-referential self-improving agents optimizing any computable task.
- **letta (★25k)** — stateful agents: memory as the substrate of self-improvement (archival + core memory editing).
- **DGM (Darwin Gödel Machine, Sakana AI, arXiv:2502.15145)** — self-referential agent rewrites its own tools/prompts with an archive of agents and checkpoint-before-mutation; documented objective-hacking when subjective evaluation diverges from the objective metric. (Named here from prior study + q04 receipts; not re-fetched this wave.)
- **Sober evaluation refs** — FML-Bench (arXiv:2605.17373) and AIRA-Dojo (arXiv:2507.02554): community spend on agent optimization with "limited algorithmic progress to show for it" (weco's own citation).

## §3 The deeper abstractions (the synergy layer)

Strip the branding and every system above is the same loop at a different altitude: **generate → evaluate (cost-constrained, partly held-out) → select → write the lesson into durable state → repeat, with the loop eventually pointing at its own harness.** The disagreements are about WHERE the loop closes and WHAT the durable state is:

1. **The outer loop is the product** (weco). Not the agent — the loop that rewrites the agent. Our analog exists and is already receipted: PLANNING.md refinement loop = outer loop; lanes = inner loops; worklog = the loop's durable state. What weco adds: the outer loop should have its OWN evaluation, not just taste.
2. **A private score is the only anti-hacking engine that works** (AIDE², DGM's hacking findings). Selection pressure from a held-out score the optimizer cannot see produced anti-hacking machinery NOBODY asked for. Our analog: pre-registered seals + stranger verification + the two-reader rule are literally private scores relative to the running system — but we can make this precise (M3).
3. **Fixed budgets force algorithmic invention** (AIDE²: brute force cannot pass). This is an independent theoretical justification of our pricing-first law and $0.05 lane caps: the cap is not thrift, it is selection pressure for laws over scale.
4. **Language is the learning medium; lessons are the gradient** (GEPA). Text reflection + Pareto-frontier lesson combination beat scalar-gradient methods per unit cost on agentic tasks. Our worklog lessons are prose; they should be objects with evidence links (M2).
5. **Durable state, not context** (prime-agent Continual Harness, letta, reef). Self-improvement = small, evidence-backed, versioned updates to persistent state (prompts/skills/specs) — not bigger context windows. AIDE²'s 16× COMPRESSION converges with this: the winner cut context, it did not grow it.
6. **Archive, not population** (AlphaEvolve/DGM vs the AIDE² rejection). Under a fixed budget, island populations with migration UNDERPERFORMED a single greedy-with-bandit-lineage loop. The useful residue is the ARCHIVE (diverse best-known artifacts kept addressable), not the population (parallel spend). Directly relevant to how the fleet spends: lanes ≠ islands in the GA sense; the archive of laws is the asset.
7. **Self-play task generation** (Absolute Zero): the proposer/solver split turns verification capacity into new training signal. For a fleet whose verification capacity is its signature strength, this is the natural gift-engine (M6).
8. **Mutation fragility is a first-class failure mode** (AIDE²'s broken anti-hacking layer; our e_a11 predecessor-regression practice). Any seal that changes a mechanism must re-verify the mechanisms it stands on — already fleet practice in the A-chain; generalize it (M7).
9. **Autonomy has rungs, and honesty about the rung is the progress metric** (weco L0–L3; Metan's five rungs). Both frameworks price "we are at rung X, rung X+1 requires Y" as the honest unit of progress. Our PLANNING refinements already do this informally; the ladder gives it a shared vocabulary with the field.
10. **Simple baselines discipline** (the counter-paper): any evolved pipeline must be compared to simple baselines under matched budget. Our dose-response ladders and null controls are this. Keep it as a registration requirement (M4).

## §5 The system to find them — lode

Breakthroughs are not found by reading more; they are found by a loop that converts reading into SEALED, FALSIFIABLE, PRICED bets and folds the verdicts back. `lode/` (this repo) is that loop, born with: protocol (scout → extract → score → register → lane → fold), an append-only mines ledger with sha256-sealed predictions, the fleet experiment registry (mid-arc objective #11 fulfilled), an Elo triage tournament with disclosed keeper judgment, and fail-closed validation with tamper self-tests. Every PLANNING refinement from wave 60 on cites the mines it consumed or rejects them with reasons — the outer loop now has an evaluation of its own.
